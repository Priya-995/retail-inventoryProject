## Step 2 — Product metadata normalisation
Applies to all 10 columns listed below:

| Field | Rule applied |
|---|---|
| `category` | Mode of per-product source rows (stable tie-break: sorted mode values) |
| `supplier_id` | Mode of per-product source rows (same tie-break) |
| `unit_cost` | Median of per-product source rows, rounded to 2 d.p. |
| `reorder_level` | Median of per-product source rows, rounded to nearest integer, min=1 |
| `lead_days` | Median of per-product source rows, rounded to nearest integer, min=1 |

After normalisation each product carries identical metadata values across all
its rows. The 5 normalised fields are therefore **product-constant** in
`data/dataset.csv`.

**Demand generation rules (illustrative, not real-world observations):**
- Base daily-sales range: Grocery 8–45, Electronics 2–18, HomeDecor 2–20,
  Apparel 3–25 units.
- Summer multiplier (Apr–Jun): ×1.20.
- Diwali window multiplier (Oct 20 – Nov 5): ×1.40.
- Weekend multiplier (Sat/Sun): ×1.08.
- Gaussian noise: `N(1.0, 0.18)` applied to demand before rounding.
- `stock_qty` derived from `daily_sales × coverage_days + N(0,12)` where
  `coverage_days ~ Uniform(1.5, 18)`.

Month-weighted date sampling: April–June and October–November receive higher
weights (×1.20 and ×1.10 respectively) before normalisation.
